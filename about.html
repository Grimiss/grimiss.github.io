Behind Grimiss Ambient

Where the
journey started.

After moving from England to Denmark in 2020, my first tape-machine purchase from a genbrugsbutik — a Danish second-hand shop — was a small shoebox cassette recorder.

I modified it with a speed control and an input control. That simple machine became a way to experiment with sound, and helped reignite my passion for making music with analogue tape.

From that shoebox recorder, the collection grew to include reel-to-reel machines, cassette decks and classic hi-fi equipment, alongside modern synths and digital production. An old Bang & Olufsen reel-to-reel recorder became another part of that journey.

A collection.
A way of listening.

I enjoy the hands-on side of these machines: changing tape speed, exploring a worn cassette and finding something unexpected in an ordinary sound. Each recorder brings its own character to the music.

That curiosity became Grimiss Ambient. I make slowly evolving soundscapes for focus, relaxation and sleep, with room for the small imperfections that make a recording feel alive.

For me, tape gives the music a sense of movement and familiarity. You can hear the results in the Listening Room, or follow the journey from computer to tape on the Process page.

From listening to creating

The Sound Factory.

The same fascination with tape is also behind The Sound Factory, my upcoming environment for creating soundscapes. Its first instrument, Drone Machine, brings four-track tape-loop recording and sound shaping together.
